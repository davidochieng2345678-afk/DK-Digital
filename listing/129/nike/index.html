The Nike Story: From Blue Ribbon to Global Icon

Discover the remarkable journey of how two visionary entrepreneurs built the world's most iconic sports brand.

The Birth of a Legend: Blue Ribbon Sports (1964)

Nike's story begins in 1964 when University of Oregon track coach Bill Bowerman and his former middle-distance runner Phil Knight joined forces to create Blue Ribbon Sports. With a handshake and a dream, they invested $500 each to import high-quality running shoes from Japan under the Onitsuka Tiger brand. The company operated out of the trunk of Knight's car, selling shoes at track meets across the Pacific Northwest.

Bowerman, a legendary coach who had trained 31 Olympians, was constantly experimenting with shoe designs to give his athletes an edge. His relentless pursuit of performance innovation would become the cornerstone of Nike's philosophy. Knight, a Stanford MBA graduate, brought the business acumen and global vision that would transform a small startup into a billion-dollar empire.

The Swoosh: A $35 Logo That Changed the World

In 1971, Phil Knight approached a young graphic design student at Portland State University named Carolyn Davidson to create a logo for the new brand. He wanted something "dynamic, fluid, and athletic." Davidson spent 17.5 hours designing the now-iconic Swoosh — a simple, elegant curve that conveyed speed, motion, and victory. She was paid $35 for her work and later received $500 in Nike stock, which became worth millions.

The Swoosh, inspired by the wing of the Greek goddess Nike (the goddess of victory), became one of the most recognized logos on Earth. It represents the brand's core values: performance, excellence, and the relentless pursuit of greatness.

"Just Do It": The Most Powerful Slogan in Sports

In 1988, Nike launched one of the most memorable marketing campaigns in history. The "Just Do It" slogan, inspired by the final words of convicted murderer Gary Gilmore ("Let's do it"), was adapted by advertising executive Dan Wieden of Wieden+Kennedy. Wieden believed the phrase captured the determination, grit, and can-do spirit that defined athletes and everyday people alike.

The campaign's first television commercial featured Walt Stack, an 80-year-old marathon runner, jogging across the Golden Gate Bridge. It was simple, authentic, and powerful. "Just Do It" transcended sports to become a global cultural phenomenon, inspiring millions to push beyond their limits and embrace action over hesitation.

Air Jordan: The Partnership That Changed Everything

In 1984, Nike took a massive gamble that would define the brand for generations. They signed a rookie basketball player named Michael Jordan to a $2.5 million endorsement deal — an astronomical sum at the time. The Air Jordan 1 sneaker was born, and with it, a cultural revolution.

The NBA initially banned the red-and-black sneakers, calling them "not in uniform guidelines." Nike leaned into the controversy with a brilliant marketing campaign: "The NBA can't stop you from wearing them." Sales exploded. The Air Jordan line became a multi-billion dollar empire, transcending basketball to become a global fashion phenomenon.

Today, the Jordan Brand is a subsidiary of Nike, generating over $5 billion in annual revenue. It remains one of the most successful athlete partnerships in history, with 34 signature shoe models and counting.

Nike Air: The Cushioning Revolution

Nike Air is the brand's most iconic cushioning technology, introduced in 1978 by aerospace engineer Marion Franklin Rudy. Rudy's innovative idea was to place pressurized gas-filled bags in the midsole of running shoes to provide superior shock absorption and energy return. The technology debuted in the Nike Tailwind and has since evolved into multiple iterations, including Nike Air Max (visible Air), Nike Air Zoom (low-profile responsive cushioning), and Nike Air VaporMax (fully independent Air units).

Today, Nike Air is used in over 200 shoe models across running, basketball, football, and lifestyle categories. The technology has been continuously refined through advanced material science and biomechanical testing, making it one of the most studied and validated cushioning systems in sports history.

Flyknit: The Future of Footwear Construction

Launched in 2012, Nike Flyknit revolutionized shoe manufacturing by creating seamless, lightweight uppers through computer-knitting technology. The process uses precision knitting to create a single-piece upper that fits like a second skin, providing targeted support, breathability, and flexibility exactly where it's needed.

Flyknit reduces waste by up to 60% compared to traditional cut-and-sew methods, making it both performance-enhancing and environmentally sustainable. The technology has been adopted across running, basketball, football, and lifestyle footwear, with models like the Nike Flyknit Racer, Nike Flyknit Trainer, and Nike Flyknit Air Max becoming instant classics.

Dri-FIT: Stay Dry, Stay Comfortable

Nike Dri-FIT is the brand's moisture-wicking fabric technology, designed to draw sweat away from the skin to the fabric's surface, where it evaporates quickly. This keeps athletes dry, comfortable, and focused on their performance. The technology uses microfiber polyester and advanced knit construction to achieve its effects.

Dri-FIT is used across Nike's entire apparel line, from running tops and training tights to football jerseys and basketball uniforms. The brand has also developed specialized versions like Dri-FIT ADV (advanced moisture management) and Dri-FIT UV (sun protection), ensuring that athletes are protected and performant in any condition.

React & Foam Technologies: Next-Level Energy Return

Nike React is the brand's latest foam technology, designed to provide unprecedented energy return, softness, and durability. React foam is engineered at the molecular level to deliver a springy, responsive feel that keeps athletes moving efficiently. The technology debuted in the Nike Epic React running shoe and has since been adopted in basketball, training, and lifestyle footwear.

Other innovative foam technologies include Nike ZoomX (ultra-lightweight, high-energy return foam used in the Vaporfly series), Nike Lunarlon (soft and responsive), and Nike Cushlon (balanced cushioning). Together, these technologies represent decades of material science research and athlete feedback, ensuring that every runner, jumper, and athlete gets the performance they need.

Vaporfly: The Shoe That Changed Running Forever

The Nike Vaporfly series represents a quantum leap in running shoe technology. Featuring Nike ZoomX foam and a curved carbon fiber plate, the Vaporfly is scientifically proven to improve running economy by up to 4% — a game-changing advantage for elite athletes.

In 2019, Eliud Kipchoge became the first person to run a marathon in under 2 hours (1:59:40) wearing the Nike Vaporfly. The shoe has since helped athletes shatter multiple world records across distances from 5K to the marathon. The Vaporfly's success has sparked a global debate about technology in sport, cementing its place as one of the most revolutionary shoes ever created.

What is Move to Zero?

Move to Zero is Nike's comprehensive sustainability initiative designed to achieve zero carbon emissions and zero waste across the company's global operations. Launched in 2018, the program represents Nike's bold commitment to addressing climate change, reducing environmental impact, and creating a more sustainable future for the planet.

The initiative is built on three core pillars: circular design (creating products that can be recycled and repurposed), sustainable materials (using recycled and renewable resources), and carbon reduction (minimizing the brand's carbon footprint). Nike aims to reduce its carbon footprint by 50% by 2030 and achieve net-zero emissions by 2050.

Frequently Asked Questions: Everything About Nike

Got questions about Nike products, shipping, sustainability, or the brand's presence in Kenya? Find the answers here.

Q

What is Nike known for?

Nike is known for its iconic Swoosh logo, the "Just Do It" slogan, and its world-class footwear, apparel, and equipment. Key product lines include Air Max, Air Jordan, Air Force 1, and Dri-FIT apparel. Nike is also recognized for its innovation in sports technology, sustainability initiatives (Move to Zero), and its partnerships with elite athletes like Michael Jordan, Eliud Kipchoge, and LeBron James.

Q

When was Nike founded?

Nike was founded in 1964 as Blue Ribbon Sports by Bill Bowerman and Phil Knight. The company officially became Nike Inc. in 1971 when the iconic Swoosh logo was created. Nike celebrated its 60th anniversary in 2024, marking six decades of innovation, athletic excellence, and cultural impact.

Q

What is the Air Jordan brand?

Air Jordan is a subsidiary brand of Nike, created in 1984 in partnership with basketball legend Michael Jordan. The Air Jordan line features signature sneakers, apparel, and accessories that have become iconic in both sports and fashion culture. With 34 signature models and over $5 billion in annual revenue, the Jordan Brand is one of the most successful athlete partnerships in history and has transcended basketball to become a global fashion phenomenon.

Q

Does Nike ship to Kenya?

Yes, Nike offers international shipping to Kenya through its official website (nike.com) with delivery to major cities including Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, and Eldoret. Kenyan consumers can also purchase Nike products through authorized local retailers, including Nike stores in Nairobi shopping centers like Sarit Centre, Westlands, and The Junction. Online purchases can be made using credit cards, debit cards, and M-Pesa through select payment gateways.

What is Nike's sustainability initiative?

Nike's sustainability initiative is called "Move to Zero", which aims to achieve zero carbon emissions and zero waste. The brand uses recycled materials (over 70% of products contain recycled content), sustainable packaging (100% recyclable), and innovative manufacturing processes (Flyknit reduces waste by up to 60%). Nike is committed to carbon neutrality by 2025 and net-zero emissions by 2050 through renewable energy, supply chain optimization, and circular design principles.
