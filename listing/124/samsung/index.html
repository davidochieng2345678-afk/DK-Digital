The Samsung Story: From Trading Company to Global Technology Powerhouse

Discover the remarkable journey of how a small trading company in South Korea became one of the world's largest and most innovative technology conglomerates, shaping the digital landscape for over 80 years.

The Birth of a Giant: A Trading Company in Daegu (1938)

Samsung's remarkable story began in 1938 when Lee Byung-chul founded a small trading company in Daegu, South Korea. The name "Samsung" translates to "Three Stars" in Korean — representing "big, numerous, and powerful." The company initially focused on exporting dried fish, locally grown groceries, and noodles to China and other neighboring countries. Lee Byung-chul's vision was to create a business that would serve the nation and contribute to its economic growth.

Over the next two decades, Samsung diversified into textiles, insurance, and retail, establishing itself as a major player in the South Korean economy. By the 1960s, Samsung had grown into a conglomerate with a diverse portfolio of businesses. However, it was the company's bold entry into electronics in 1969 that would define its future and set the stage for its transformation into a global technology powerhouse. This move marked the beginning of a new era for Samsung, one that would eventually see it become a household name around the world.

Founded 1938 Trading Company Origins Lee Byung-chul

The Electronics Revolution: Entering a New Era (1969)

In 1969, Samsung made a pivotal decision that would change its trajectory forever: it entered the electronics industry. The company established Samsung Electronics, initially producing black-and-white televisions. This was a bold bet on the future of technology, and it paid off spectacularly. Samsung quickly expanded its electronics portfolio, moving into semiconductors, telecommunications, and consumer electronics.

The 1970s and 1980s were a period of rapid growth for Samsung Electronics. The company began exporting its products globally, building a reputation for quality and innovation. In 1983, Samsung entered the semiconductor business, a move that would propel it to global leadership. The company's first 64K DRAM chip was a milestone achievement, marking Samsung's entry into the high-tech manufacturing sector. This was followed by LCD displays, mobile phones, and other advanced technologies, establishing Samsung as a technological pioneer.

1969: Entry into Electronics First Black-and-White TV 1983: Semiconductor Entry

The Semiconductor Revolution: Building a Global Industry Leader

Samsung's entry into the semiconductor industry in the 1980s was a game-changing decision that would establish the company as a global technology leader. The company's investment in memory chip technology paid off spectacularly. By the 1990s, Samsung had become the world's largest memory chip manufacturer, a position it continues to hold today. Samsung's DRAM and NAND flash memory chips are used in countless devices worldwide, from smartphones to servers to automobiles.

Samsung's semiconductor leadership has driven innovation across the technology industry. The company has pioneered advancements in 3D NAND, EUV lithography, and high-bandwidth memory. Samsung's foundry business has also become a major player, manufacturing chips for other technology companies. Today, Samsung's semiconductor division is one of the most profitable and strategically important parts of the company, generating billions of dollars in revenue and powering the digital economy.

World's Largest Memory Chip Maker DRAM & NAND Leader Leading Foundry

The Galaxy Era: Redefining Mobile Technology (2009-Present)

In 2009, Samsung launched the Galaxy series of smartphones, marking a new chapter in the company's history. The Galaxy S was the first device to challenge Apple's iPhone, offering cutting-edge features, large displays, and powerful processors. The Galaxy series quickly became a global phenomenon, establishing Samsung as a dominant force in the smartphone market.

Since then, Samsung has continued to innovate with its Galaxy lineup. The company pioneered the phablet category with the Galaxy Note, introduced foldable phone technology with the Galaxy Z Fold and Z Flip, and has integrated 5G, advanced cameras, and AI-powered features across its devices. The Galaxy ecosystem now includes smartphones, tablets, smartwatches, wireless earbuds, and laptops, all connected through Samsung's SmartThings platform. Samsung has become the world's largest smartphone manufacturer, shipping over 300 million devices annually and shaping the future of mobile technology.

2009: Galaxy S Launch World's Largest Smartphone Maker Foldable Phone Pioneer

Display Technology & TVs: Leading the Visual Revolution

Samsung has been a pioneer in display technology for decades. The company introduced the world's first 55-inch OLED TV and has continuously pushed the boundaries of what's possible. Samsung's QLED TVs are renowned for their exceptional picture quality, brightness, and color accuracy. The company's Neo QLED and OLED technologies offer even better viewing experiences, with infinite contrast ratios and vibrant colors.

Samsung's display division supplies panels to other manufacturers, including Apple, Dell, and HP. The company's OLED panels are used in smartphones, tablets, laptops, and televisions worldwide. Samsung has also pioneered flexible and foldable displays, enabling new form factors like the Galaxy Z Fold and Z Flip. This leadership in display technology has made Samsung a critical supplier to the global electronics industry and a leader in visual innovation.

What is Samsung known for?

Samsung is known for its innovative electronics and technology including Galaxy smartphones, QLED and OLED TVs, home appliances, semiconductors, and display technology. Q

When was Samsung founded?

Samsung was founded in 1938 by Lee Byung-chul in Daegu, South Korea, as a small trading company with just 40 employees. The company started by exporting dried fish, noodles, and groceries to China and neighboring countries. Over the decades, Samsung diversified into textiles, insurance, retail, and electronics, eventually becoming one of the world's largest conglomerates. Samsung Electronics was established in 1969, marking the company's entry into the technology industry that would define its global success. Samsung celebrated its 86th anniversary in 2024, marking nearly nine decades of innovation and global leadership.

  • Galaxy A Series: Feature-packed, accessible smartphones for everyday users

  • Galaxy Buds: Wireless earbuds with premium audio quality

The Galaxy series is known for its premium design, innovative features, and cutting-edge technology, making it one of the most popular smartphone lineups in the world. Samsung is the world's largest smartphone manufacturer, shipping over 300 million devices annually.

What is Samsung's semiconductor business?

Samsung is the world's leading semiconductor manufacturer, producing memory chips and processors that power billions of devices worldwide. Key areas include:

  • DRAM (Dynamic Random-Access Memory): Used in computers, servers, and smartphones for high-speed data processing
  • NAND Flash: Used in storage devices, SSDs, and memory cards for data storage
  • V-NAND: Samsung's advanced 3D NAND technology with over 200 layers
  • Exynos Processors: Custom ARM-based chips for Samsung's Galaxy devices
  • Foundry: Samsung manufactures chips for other companies, including industry leaders
  • Automotive Semiconductors: Chips for self-driving cars, infotainment, and advanced driver assistance

Samsung's semiconductor division generates billions of dollars in revenue and is a critical supplier to the global technology industry. The company invests over $25 billion annually in R&D to maintain its leadership position.
