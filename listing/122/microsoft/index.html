The Microsoft Story: From a Garage to a Global Technology Powerhouse

Discover the remarkable journey of how two childhood friends built a company that would revolutionize personal computing, shape the internet era, and lead the world into the age of artificial intelligence.

The Birth of a Giant: A Garage in Albuquerque (1975)

Microsoft's story began in 1975 when two childhood friends — Bill Gates (19) and Paul Allen (22) — read about the Altair 8800, one of the first personal computers, in Popular Electronics magazine. Gates and Allen saw an opportunity: the Altair needed software to be useful. They contacted MITS, the Altair's manufacturer, and claimed they had developed a BASIC interpreter for the machine — even though they hadn't written a line of code. They spent the next few weeks working tirelessly in a garage in Albuquerque, New Mexico, and delivered a working version. The Altair BASIC interpreter was a huge success, and Microsoft (originally "Micro-Soft," a combination of "microcomputer" and "software") was born.

The company's first office was a small room in Albuquerque, and its first employees were just a handful of programmers. Gates dropped out of Harvard University to focus on the company full-time. Microsoft's early years were marked by intense competition and rapid growth. The company's BASIC interpreter became the industry standard for personal computers, and Microsoft established itself as a leading software developer. The company moved to Bellevue, Washington in 1979, and later to its current headquarters in Redmond, setting the stage for its global dominance.

Founded 1975 Altair BASIC Gates & Allen

The IBM Deal: MS-DOS and the PC Revolution (1980-1981)

In 1980, Microsoft made a pivotal deal that would define its future and shape the entire personal computer industry. IBM, the dominant computer company of the era, was developing its first personal computer and needed an operating system. Microsoft, which didn't have an operating system at the time, purchased QDOS (Quick and Dirty Operating System) from Seattle Computer Products for $50,000, modified it, and licensed it to IBM as PC-DOS (which was later sold to other PC manufacturers as MS-DOS).

Crucially, Microsoft retained the rights to license MS-DOS to other computer manufacturers. This brilliant business decision allowed Microsoft to dominate the operating system market as the PC industry exploded. By 1983, Microsoft's revenue had grown to $55 million, and the company was well on its way to becoming a technology powerhouse. MS-DOS became the foundation of the PC industry, and Microsoft's business model of selling software licenses to hardware manufacturers became the industry standard.

1980: IBM Deal MS-DOS $50,000 Purchase

The Windows Revolution: Redefining Personal Computing (1985-1995)

In 1985, Microsoft released Windows 1.0, a graphical user interface that ran on top of MS-DOS. While the initial version was not a commercial success, it was the beginning of a revolution. Over the next decade, Microsoft iterated and improved Windows, releasing Windows 2.0 (1987), Windows 3.0 (1990), and Windows 3.1 (1992), each version gaining momentum and popularity.

The real breakthrough came in 1995 with the release of Windows 95. It was a complete overhaul of the operating system, featuring the Start menu, taskbar, and plug-and-play support. Windows 95 was a massive success, with over 40 million copies sold in its first year. The iconic launch, featuring the Rolling Stones' "Start Me Up" campaign, cemented Microsoft's dominance in the PC market. Windows became the world's most widely used operating system, a position it continues to hold today, with over 1.5 billion users worldwide.

1985: Windows 1.0 1995: Windows 95 1.5B+ Users

Microsoft Office: The Productivity Empire (1989-Present)

In 1989, Microsoft launched Microsoft Office, a suite of productivity applications including Word, Excel, and PowerPoint. These tools became the global standard for business productivity, and Microsoft Office (now Microsoft 365) remains the most widely used productivity suite in the world.

Microsoft Office has evolved dramatically over the years. The introduction of Microsoft 365 (formerly Office 365) shifted the model from one-time purchases to subscription-based cloud services, including OneDrive cloud storage, Teams collaboration, and AI-powered features. Today, Microsoft 365 has over 345 million paid users and is used by millions of businesses, schools, and individuals worldwide. The suite's integration with cloud services and AI capabilities has made it essential for modern work and learning.

1989: Microsoft Office Microsoft 365 345M+ Paid Users

The Cloud Transformation: Microsoft Azure (2010-Present)

In 2010, Microsoft launched Azure, its cloud computing platform, marking a major strategic shift for the company. Azure was Microsoft's response to the cloud revolution led by Amazon Web Services (AWS), and it quickly became a cornerstone of Microsoft's business.

Azure provides computing, storage, networking, and AI services to businesses and developers worldwide. It is now one of the world's leading cloud platforms, competing with AWS and Google Cloud. Azure's enterprise focus, hybrid capabilities, and integration with Microsoft's software stack have made it a top choice for large organizations. Today, Azure generates over $60 billion in annual revenue and is a key driver of Microsoft's growth. Azure's AI services, including Azure OpenAI Service, have made it a leader in AI-powered cloud computing.

2010: Azure Launched $60B+ Annual Revenue AI-Powered Cloud

Bill Gates (born 1955) and Paul Allen (1953-2018) founded Microsoft in 1975 with a vision to put a computer on every desk and in every home. Gates' relentless drive, business acumen, and programming brilliance were matched by Allen's technical foresight and entrepreneurial spirit. Together, they transformed the technology industry, making personal computing accessible to billions and creating one of the most valuable companies in history. Their legacy lives on in every Windows PC, every Microsoft 365 user, and the countless lives that have been empowered by Microsoft technology. Gates' subsequent philanthropy through the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation has continued to change the world, addressing global health, education, and poverty.

What is Microsoft known for?

Microsoft is known for its Windows operating system, the world's most widely used OS, as well as its Microsoft 365 productivity suite (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, Teams), the Azure cloud platform, Surface devices, and Xbox gaming consoles. Q

When was Microsoft founded?

Microsoft was founded in 1975 by Bill Gates and Paul Allen in Albuquerque, New Mexico. The company's first product was a BASIC interpreter for the Altair 8800 microcomputer. Microsoft moved to Bellevue, Washington in 1979 and later to its current headquarters in Redmond, Washington. The company celebrated its 49th anniversary in 2024, marking nearly five decades of innovation, global leadership, and technological transformation.

Microsoft's IPO in 1986 at $21 per share raised $61 million, making Bill Gates a billionaire and creating thousands of millionaires. Under the leadership of Satya Nadella (CEO since 2014), Microsoft has undergone a transformation focused on cloud computing, AI, and a growth mindset culture, becoming one of the world's most valuable companies with a market cap exceeding $3 trillion.

📅 1975 👥 Gates & Allen 🏢 Redmond, WA 💰 $3T+ Market Cap

What is Microsoft Azure?

Microsoft Azure is a cloud computing platform that provides computing, storage, networking, and AI services to businesses and developers worldwide. Launched in 2010, Azure is one of the world's leading cloud platforms, competing with AWS and Google Cloud. Azure is used by 95% of Fortune 500 companies and generates over $60 billion in annual revenue.

Azure offers over 200 products and services, including:

  • Computing: Virtual machines, containers, serverless computing
  • Storage: Blob storage, file storage, data lakes
  • AI & Machine Learning: Azure OpenAI Service, machine learning, cognitive services
  • Networking: Virtual networks, firewalls, content delivery
  • Databases: SQL, NoSQL, Cosmos DB
  • Developer Tools: DevOps, GitHub integration, Visual Studio

Azure has 60+ data center regions worldwide, including South Africa, providing low-latency cloud services to Kenyan businesses and organizations.

What is Microsoft's sustainability commitment?

Microsoft has set ambitious sustainability goals to address climate change and reduce its environmental impact:

  • Water positive: By 2030, Microsoft will replenish more water than it consumes
  • Zero waste: By 2030, Microsoft will generate zero waste across its operations
  • Sustainable packaging: Eliminating single-use plastics and using sustainable materials

Microsoft's sustainability initiatives are also active in Kenya, with investments in renewable energy, digital skills training, and community development. The company's AI for Good program supports environmental projects and sustainable development across Africa.
