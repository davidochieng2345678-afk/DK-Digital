Step 1: Identify Your Best-Performing Content

🔍 The Discovery: Finding the hidden gems in your content library that have the most traffic potential

You can't multiply what you don't know exists. The first step to 10x'ing your traffic is identifying your best-performing content — the pieces that are already getting traction and have the most potential to explode with a little optimization.

Most businesses have hidden gems. Content that's getting some traffic but could get 10x more with the right strategy. Content that's almost ranking but just needs a push. Content that's already converting but not getting enough eyeballs.

In this section, I'll show you exactly how to find those gems — using data, not guesswork.

📊 Step 1.1: Use Google Analytics to Find Your Top Pages

Google Analytics is your first stop. It shows you exactly which pages are getting the most traffic — and which ones have the most potential.

What to Look For:

  • Top pages by pageviews: Which pages get the most visitors? These are your traffic drivers.
  • Top pages by unique pageviews: Which pages attract the most new visitors?
  • Top pages by time on page: Which pages keep people engaged the longest?
  • Top pages by conversions: Which pages drive leads or sales? This is the most important metric.
  • Top pages by bounce rate: Which pages have the lowest bounce rate? These pages are resonating with visitors.

📋 How to Find Top Pages in Google Analytics:

  1. Log into Google Analytics
  2. Go to Behavior → Site Content → All Pages
  3. Sort by Pageviews (or Unique Pageviews)
  4. Export the top 50-100 pages to a spreadsheet
  5. Add columns for conversions, bounce rate, and time on page
  6. Score each page based on these metrics

✅ Pro Tip:

Set up goals in Google Analytics to track conversions. This way, you'll know exactly which pages are driving leads and sales — not just traffic.

🔍 Step 1.2: Use Google Search Console to Find Your Ranking Potential

Google Search Console shows you what's already working in search. It reveals which pages are already ranking — and which ones are almost there.

What to Look For:

  • Top pages by impressions: Which pages are showing up in search results? These pages have potential to get more clicks.
  • Top pages by clicks: Which pages are already getting traffic from search? These are your winners.
  • Pages with high impressions but low CTR: These pages are showing up but not getting clicks — a title tag or meta description update could double their traffic.
  • Pages ranking on page 2: Pages ranking on page 2 are close to page 1 — a few tweaks could push them over the edge.
  • Keywords you're ranking for: What keywords are you already ranking for? These are opportunities to optimize further.

📋 How to Find Ranking Potential in Search Console:

  1. Log into Google Search Console
  2. Go to Performance → Search Results
  3. Sort by Impressions or Clicks
  4. Export the data to a spreadsheet
  5. Look for pages with high impressions but low CTR
  6. Look for pages ranking on page 2 (positions 11-20)

💡 The "Page 2" Opportunity:

Pages ranking on page 2 (positions 11-20) are your biggest opportunities. A few small optimizations — better title tags, more internal links, improved content — can push them to page 1 and 10x their traffic.

📱 Step 1.3: Check Social Media Engagement

Your audience is already telling you what they like. Social media engagement reveals which content resonates with your audience.

What to Look For:

  • Top posts by shares: Shares are the strongest signal of value. People share content they find useful.
  • Top posts by comments: Comments show engagement and interest. People comment on content they care about.
  • Top posts by likes: Likes show appreciation — content that resonates with your audience.
  • Top posts by saves: Saves (on Instagram, LinkedIn) show long-term value — people want to come back to it.
  • Content that sparked conversations: Which posts generated the most discussion?

📋 Where to Check:

  • Facebook — Page Insights → Posts
  • Instagram — Insights → Content
  • LinkedIn — Analytics → Posts
  • X (Twitter) — Analytics → Tweets
  • Pinterest — Analytics → Top Pins

💰 Step 1.4: Analyse Conversion Data

Traffic is nice — but conversions are everything. The content that actually drives sales or leads is your most valuable content.

What to Look For:

  • Pages that generate leads: Which pages drive the most form submissions, newsletter signups, or consultation requests?
  • Pages that drive sales: Which pages lead to the most purchases or revenue?
  • Pages with high conversion rates: Which pages convert at the highest percentage? These are your best performers.
  • Pages that assist conversions: Which pages are part of the conversion path (even if they're not the final page)? These pages are building trust and moving people toward conversion.

✅ Pro Tip:

Set up conversion tracking in Google Analytics. This will show you exactly which pages are driving revenue — so you can double down on what works.

🔗 Step 1.5: Check Backlinks

Backlinks are a sign of quality. If other websites are linking to your content, it's valuable — and worth optimizing and repurposing.

What to Look For:

  • Pages with the most backlinks: Which pages have the most external links? These are your most authoritative pages.
  • Pages with high-quality backlinks: Which pages have links from authoritative websites?
  • Pages with backlinks from Kenyan sites: Which pages have local authority?
  • Pages with link growth: Which pages are attracting new links? These pages have momentum.

📋 How to Check Backlinks:

  • Ahrefs — Site Explorer → Best by Links
  • SEMrush — Backlink Analytics
  • Google Search Console — Links → Top Linked Pages
  • Moz — Link Explorer

Step 2: Optimize Your Content for More Keywords

📝 The Expansion: Turning one ranking keyword into five — multiplying your search traffic without writing new content

Your content can rank for more than one keyword. Most pages are under-optimized — ranking for 1-2 keywords when they could be ranking for 5, 10, or even 20 related keywords.

The Kiruo Traffic Multiplier optimizes every page to capture every possible keyword that's relevant to your content. By expanding your keyword footprint, you can multiply your search traffic without writing a single new word.

In this section, I'll show you exactly how to optimize your existing content for more keywords — using proven techniques that deliver measurable results.

🔍 Step 2.1: Find Related Keywords

Before you can optimize, you need to know what to optimize for. Start by finding related keywords that your page could rank for.

How to Find Related Keywords:

  • Use Google Autocomplete: Start typing your main keyword in Google and see what suggestions appear. These are related searches that people are actually using.
  • Use "People Also Ask": Search for your main keyword and look at the "People Also Ask" section — these are question-based keywords.
  • Use keyword research tools: SEMrush, Ahrefs, or Ubersuggest can show you all the keywords your page could rank for.
  • Check competitor pages: See what keywords your competitors are ranking for that you're missing.
  • Use Google Search Console: See what keywords you're already ranking for — and find more like them.

📋 Keyword Research Example:

Main Keyword:

"coffee shop Kilimani"

Related Keywords Found:

  • "best coffee Kilimani"
  • "coffee near me Kilimani"
  • "Kilimani cafe"
  • "breakfast Kilimani"
  • "coffee shop near Yaya Centre"
  • "best coffee in Nairobi"

Result: The page can now target 6+ keywords instead of just 1.

📋 Step 2.2: Update Your Title Tag

Your title tag is the most important on-page SEO element. Updating it to include more keywords can significantly increase your click-through rate and expand your keyword footprint.

Title Tag Best Practices:

  • Include your primary keyword — Place it near the beginning of the title.
  • Include 1-2 secondary keywords — Related keywords that capture additional search traffic.
  • Keep it under 60 characters — Longer titles get cut off in search results.
  • Make it compelling — Use power words, numbers, and benefits to increase click-through rate.
  • Include your location — For local businesses, include neighborhood and city names.

📝 Title Tag Before & After:

❌ Before:

"Coffee Shop in Kilimani"

Targets 1 keyword

✅ After:

"Best Coffee Shop in Kilimani | Coffee Near Me & Breakfast"

Targets 4+ keywords

📝 Step 2.3: Update Your Meta Description

Your meta description doesn't directly affect rankings — but it affects CTR. A compelling meta description with relevant keywords can double your click-through rate and bring in more traffic from the same rankings.

Meta Description Best Practices:

  • Include primary and secondary keywords — Keywords in the description are bolded in search results, attracting attention.
  • Keep it under 160 characters — Longer descriptions get cut off.
  • Include a call to action — "Learn more", "Get started", "Visit us today".
  • Make it specific — Tell people exactly what they'll find on the page.
  • Include location — For local businesses, include neighborhood references.

📝 Meta Description Before & After:

❌ Before:

"We are a coffee shop in Kilimani serving great coffee."

✅ After:

"Visit the best coffee shop in Kilimani. Enjoy freshly roasted coffee, delicious breakfast, and free Wi-Fi. Rated 4.9/5. Open 7am-10pm. Come taste the difference!"

📖 Step 2.4: Add New Sections to Your Content

Adding new sections to your existing content is one of the most effective ways to rank for more keywords. It gives you an opportunity to target related keywords naturally.

What New Sections to Add:

  • FAQ section: Answer common questions related to your topic. Each question and answer can target a specific keyword.
  • "How to" section: Provide a step-by-step guide related to your topic. This targets educational keywords.
  • "Best of" section: List the best options, tools, or resources related to your topic. This targets comparison keywords.
  • Local section: For local businesses, add a section about your neighborhood, community involvement, or local events. This targets local keywords.
  • Case study section: Share a real example of how your product or service helped someone. This targets trust-building keywords.

📊 New Sections Example:

❌ Before:

A blog post about "coffee shop in Kilimani" with only 1 section.

✅ After:

  • FAQ: "Where is the best coffee in Kilimani?"
  • FAQ: "What coffee shops are near Yaya Centre?"
  • How to: "How to find the perfect coffee in Kilimani"
  • Local: "Kilimani's coffee culture"
  • Best of: "Best coffee shops in Kilimani"

Now targets 5x more keywords

🔗 Step 2.5: Update Your Internal Links

Internal links help Google understand your content. By adding internal links with keyword-rich anchor text, you can reinforce your keyword targeting and distribute authority to your most important pages.

Internal Linking Best Practices:

  • Link from other pages: Add links to your optimized page from other relevant pages on your site.
  • Use keyword-rich anchor text: Instead of "click here", use descriptive anchor text that includes keywords.
  • Link to supporting content: Add links to related blog posts, product pages, or service pages that reinforce your content.
  • Update your navigation: If the page is important, consider adding it to your main navigation or footer.
  • Use breadcrumbs: Breadcrumbs help Google understand your site structure and improve internal linking.

💡 Pro Tip:

Update old blog posts to link to your optimized pages. This distributes authority to your new pages and improves your site's overall SEO.

🖼️ Step 2.6: Update Images & Alt Text

Images can also rank in search results. By optimizing your images with keyword-rich alt text and file names, you can capture additional traffic from image search.

Image Optimization Best Practices:

  • Add descriptive alt text: Include keywords in your image alt text (but keep it natural and descriptive).
  • Use descriptive file names: Instead of "IMG_1234.jpg", use "coffee-shop-kilimani.jpg".
  • Compress images: Use optimized, compressed images to improve page speed.
  • Use modern formats: Convert images to WebP for faster loading.
  • Add image captions: Captions can include additional keywords and improve user experience.

Turn your content into a downloadable checklist or template for lead generation.

How to do it:
