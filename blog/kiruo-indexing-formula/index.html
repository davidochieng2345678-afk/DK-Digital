📉 Why Most Pages Don't Get Indexed Quickly

Understanding why pages fail to index is the first step to fixing the problem. Here's what happens behind the scenes:

The "Discovered - Currently Not Indexed" Problem

This status in Google Search Console is one of the most frustrating experiences for website owners. The search result explains:

"This status usually indicates a Quality issue, not a technical error. Googlebot found the URL but decided it wasn't worth the resources to crawl and index it at that moment."

In other words, Google found your page but chose not to index it. Common reasons include:

Issue Why It Happens
Thin content Pages with little unique value don't justify the indexing cost
Duplicate content URLs that don't offer new information compared to existing pages
Technical blockers Noindex tags, robots.txt blocks, or redirect chains
Poor internal linking Google can't find the page through your site's link structure

Crawl Budget vs. Index Budget

Most website owners don't understand this distinction:

  • Crawl Budget is the number of URLs Googlebot is willing to crawl on your site in a given timeframe
  • Index Budget is the number of pages Google deems worthy of keeping in its index

In 2026, "most e-commerce sites suffer from Index Budget issues (bloat) rather than Crawl Budget issues." This means Google is visiting your site but choosing not to index many of the pages it finds — often because they're low quality or duplicate.

📢 Pillar 1: Direct Communication — Tell Google About Your Pages

Google provides official tools that let you directly tell it about your pages. These are the most powerful and fastest ways to get indexed.

The Kiruo Indexing Formula starts here. Before you do anything else, you need to establish direct communication with Google. This means:

  • Submitting your sitemap to Google Search Console
  • Using the URL Inspection tool to request indexing for individual pages
  • Monitoring your coverage reports to see what's indexed and what's not

1.1 Submit Your Sitemap to Google Search Console

Your sitemap is a roadmap that tells Google about all the pages on your site. Submitting it is the first step in getting indexed.

How to do it:

  • Log into Google Search Console
  • Go to Sitemaps in the left sidebar
  • Enter your sitemap URL (e.g., sitemap.xml)
  • Click Submit

✅ Pro Tip:

Create separate sitemaps for different content types (e.g., sitemap-posts.xml, sitemap-pages.xml, sitemap-products.xml). This helps Google prioritize crawling.

1.2 Use the URL Inspection Tool to Request Indexing

This is the fastest way to get a specific page indexed. The URL Inspection tool lets you directly ask Google to crawl and index a page.

How to do it:

  • Log into Google Search Console
  • Paste your URL into the URL Inspection bar at the top
  • Click Request Indexing
  • Wait for Google to process your request (usually 24-48 hours)

💡 Pro Tip:

Request indexing for 2-3 priority pages each day. Google limits how many requests you can make, so focus on your most important pages first.

1.3 Monitor Your Coverage Reports

Your coverage report shows you exactly which pages are indexed and which ones aren't. It's your dashboard for indexing success.

What to look for:

  • Valid (indexed): Pages that are successfully indexed
  • Excluded: Pages that aren't indexed (with reasons why)
  • Crawled - currently not indexed: Pages that Google found but chose not to index — these need your attention

📡 Pillar 2: Content Signals — Show Google Your Pages Matter

Google needs to know that your pages are valuable. If you don't send strong signals that your content is worth indexing, Google will deprioritize it.

Content signals tell Google:

  • Your pages are important — through internal linking
  • Your content is fresh — through regular updates
  • Your pages are discoverable — through sitemaps and navigation
  • Your content is high-quality — through depth and relevance

2.1 Build a Strong Internal Linking Structure

Internal links are the #1 way Google discovers new pages. If your pages are orphans (no internal links pointing to them), Google may never find them.

How to build strong internal links:

  • Link from high-authority pages (homepage, pillar pages) to new pages
  • Use descriptive anchor text that tells Google what the linked page is about
  • Create topic clusters — a pillar page linking to multiple related articles
  • Add breadcrumb navigation — this creates internal links on every page

🔗 Quick Win:

Add a "Related Posts" or "Recommended Reading" section to every blog post. This automatically creates internal links to other pages on your site.

2.2 Create Fresh Content Regularly

Google loves fresh content. Websites that publish regularly are crawled more frequently and get their new pages indexed faster.

How to create fresh content:

  • Publish new blog posts weekly (at minimum)
  • Update old posts with new information, statistics, and examples
  • Add new pages for new products, services, or topics
  • Refresh your homepage with new content regularly

2.3 Use Your XML Sitemap Effectively

Your sitemap is a content signal. It tells Google which pages are most important and how often they're updated.

Best practices for sitemaps:

  • Keep your sitemap under 50,000 URLs (Google's limit per sitemap)
  • Update your sitemap whenever you add or remove pages
  • Use priority tags (priority) to indicate which pages are most important
  • Use lastmod tags (lastmod) to show when pages were last updated

⚙️ Pillar 3: Technical Optimization — Remove Indexing Barriers

Even with great content and direct communication, technical issues can block indexing. The Kiruo Indexing Formula removes every barrier that Google might have.

Technical barriers include:

  • Noindex tags — telling Google not to index your pages
  • Robots.txt blocks — preventing Google from crawling your pages
  • Redirect errors — confusing Google with broken redirects
  • Crawl errors — pages that Google can't access
  • Slow page speed — pages that take too long to load

3.1 Check for Noindex Tags

Noindex tags tell Google not to index your pages. If you have them on important pages, they'll never rank.

How to check:

  • View your page source (Ctrl+U)
  • Search for noindex
  • If you find <meta name="robots" content="noindex">, remove it

3.2 Check Your Robots.txt File

Your robots.txt file tells Google which parts of your site to crawl and which to avoid. If you've accidentally blocked important pages, they won't be indexed.

How to check:

  • Go to yourdomain.com/robots.txt
  • Look for Disallow: lines
  • If important directories are disallowed, remove those lines

3.3 Fix Redirect Errors

Redirect errors confuse Google. If you have broken or circular redirects, Google may give up on crawling your pages.

How to fix:

  • Use a tool like Screaming Frog to find redirect chains
  • Replace redirect chains with direct 301 redirects
  • Test redirects with Google's URL Inspection tool

3.4 Fix Crawl Errors

Crawl errors happen when Google can't access your pages. This could be due to server errors, broken links, or timeouts.

How to fix:

  • Check your Coverage report in Google Search Console
  • Look for Error and Excluded pages
  • Fix any 404 errors (broken pages) with redirects
  • Fix 500 errors (server issues) with your hosting provider

Step-by-Step Implementation Guide

📋 The Action Plan: A 10-step process to get your pages indexed within 48 hours

The Kiruo Indexing Formula works — but only if you implement it correctly. This section provides a step-by-step action plan that you can follow to get your pages indexed in 48 hours or less.

Follow these steps in order. Each step builds on the previous one, and skipping steps will slow down your results.

At DK Digital, we've used this exact process to get 500+ pages indexed within 48 hours. Let's get started.

📋 Step 1: Set Up Google Search Console

Before you can do anything, you need Google Search Console. This is your command center for all indexing activities.

What to Do:

  • Go to search.google.com/search-console
  • Add your property — enter your website URL
  • Verify ownership — follow the verification instructions (HTML file upload, DNS record, or Google Analytics)
  • Wait for verification — this usually takes 1-2 minutes
  • Access your dashboard — you're now ready to use Search Console

✅ Pro Tip:

Add both domain and URL prefix properties. The domain property (e.g., example.com) gives you the most comprehensive data.

🗺️ Step 2: Submit Your XML Sitemap

Your sitemap is Google's roadmap to your pages. Submitting it ensures Google knows about every page on your site.

What to Do:

  • Create your sitemap — Most CMSs generate this automatically (WordPress with Yoast or RankMath does this for you)
  • Find your sitemap URL — usually at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml
  • Log into Search Console and go to Sitemaps
  • Enter your sitemap URL and click Submit
  • Check status — it should show "Success" within minutes

💡 Pro Tip:

Submit multiple sitemaps for different content types: sitemap-posts.xml, sitemap-pages.xml, sitemap-products.xml

🔍 Step 3: Use the URL Inspection Tool

This is the fastest way to get a page indexed. The URL Inspection tool lets you directly ask Google to crawl and index a specific page.

What to Do:

  • Log into Search Console and paste your URL into the URL Inspection bar
  • Click "Request Indexing" — this tells Google to crawl the page
  • Wait for processing — this usually takes 24-48 hours
  • Check the status — you'll see "URL is on Google" when it's indexed

📋 Which Pages to Request First:

  • Your homepage — this should be your #1 priority
  • Your most important service pages — the ones that generate revenue
  • Your newest blog posts — fresh content that needs indexing
  • Pages with "Crawled - not indexed" — fix these first

🔗 Step 4: Build Strong Internal Links

Internal links are how Google discovers new pages. If your pages have no internal links, they're orphans — and Google may never find them.

What to Do:

  • Link from your homepage to your most important pages
  • Create a "pillar page" — a comprehensive guide that links to all your related pages
  • Add "Related Posts" sections to every blog post
  • Add a footer with service links — this adds links to every page
  • Add breadcrumb navigation — this creates internal links on every page
  • Use descriptive anchor text — "Read our SEO guide" instead of "Click here"

🔗 Quick Win:

Go to your 5 most popular pages and add 3-5 internal links to newer or orphaned pages. This distributes link authority and helps Google discover new content.

🚫 Step 5: Check for Technical Barriers

Technical issues can block indexing even if you've done everything else right. You need to remove every barrier that Google might have.

What to Check:

1. Noindex Tags

  • View page source (Ctrl+U)
  • Search for noindex
  • Remove any noindex tags on important pages

2. Robots.txt

  • Go to yourdomain.com/robots.txt
  • Check for Disallow: lines
  • Remove blocks on important directories

3. Redirect Errors

  • Use Screaming Frog to find redirect chains
  • Replace with direct 301 redirects
  • Test with URL Inspection tool

4. Crawl Errors

  • Check Coverage report in Search Console
  • Fix 404s with redirects
  • Fix 500s with your hosting provider

⚡ Step 6: Optimize Page Speed

Google prioritizes fast-loading pages. If your page is slow, Google may deprioritize it for indexing.

What to Do:

  • Test your speed with Google PageSpeed Insights
  • Compress images — use TinyPNG or convert to WebP
  • Enable caching — browser caching reduces load times
  • Use a CDN — Cloudflare is a great free option
  • Minimize code — remove unnecessary CSS and JavaScript

📝 Step 7: Create Fresh Content

Google loves fresh content. Publishing regularly signals that your site is active and valuable.

What to Do:

  • Publish new blog posts — aim for 1-2 per week minimum
  • Update old posts — add new information and refresh the date
  • Add new pages — for new products, services, or topics
  • Update your homepage — add recent content or news

💡 Pro Tip:

When you update a post, change the "Last Updated" date. This signals to Google that the page is fresh and should be re-crawled.

📱 Step 8: Share on Social Media

Social signals don't directly affect indexing, but they speed up discovery. When you share a page on social media, Googlebot may crawl it sooner.

What to Do:

  • Share new content on Facebook, LinkedIn, X (Twitter), and Instagram
  • Share in relevant groups — Facebook groups, LinkedIn groups
  • Share on WhatsApp — especially for Kenyan audiences
  • Share on Medium or Quora — republish content for extra visibility

📋 Step 9: Submit to Directories

Directory submissions create backlinks that can speed up indexing. When other sites link to your pages, Googlebot follows those links and discovers your content.

What to Do:

  • Submit to local directories — Kenya Business Directory, Yellow Pages Kenya
  • Submit to niche directories — industry-specific directories
  • Submit to the DK Digital Directory — get a dofollow backlink
  • Submit to general directories — Facebook, LinkedIn, Google Business Profile

📊 Step 10: Monitor and Repeat

Indexing is an ongoing process. You need to monitor your progress and repeat the process regularly.

What to Do:

  • Check Coverage report weekly — see what's indexed and what's not
  • Request indexing for new pages as you publish them
  • Fix crawl errors as they appear
  • Update your sitemap when you add or remove pages
  • Track your indexed pages — aim for steady growth

✅ Success Checklist:

  • Your sitemap is submitted and processed
  • Your most important pages are indexed
  • Your coverage report shows minimal errors
  • Your indexed pages are growing consistently
