Mistake #1 – Crawling & Indexing Issues

Google can't rank what it can't find. If your site has crawling and indexing problems, even the best content will stay invisible. Here's how to identify and fix the most common issues affecting Kenyan websites.

Why Google Can't Find Your Pages – And How to Fix It

Imagine this: You've built a beautiful website. You've published dozens of articles. You've optimized every page. But Google never shows your content in search results.

The problem is almost always crawling and indexing. Google's bots can't access your pages, or they're blocked from indexing them. This is mistake #1 – and it's the most critical to fix.

Google bots can't crawl Blocked by robots.txt No sitemap submitted Orphan pages
Free tool: Google Search Console

⚠️ The Problem: Google Can't Find Your Content

Crawling is how Google discovers new and updated pages on your website. Indexing is how Google stores and organizes those pages in its database. If either process fails, your pages will not appear in search results.

According to our audits of 200+ Kenyan websites, 43% have crawling or indexing issues that prevent at least 30% of their pages from being indexed. That's thousands of shillings in lost potential revenue every month.

Key stat: Pages that are not indexed by Google receive zero organic traffic. Fixing crawling and indexing issues is the highest-ROI technical SEO activity.

The 4 Crawling & Indexing Mistakes:

  • 1
    Blocked by robots.txt

    Google bots are blocked from accessing your site

  • 2
    Missing or broken sitemap

    Google doesn't know which pages to crawl

  • 3
    Noindex tags on important pages

    You're telling Google NOT to index your content

  • 4
    Orphan pages (no internal links)

    Pages exist but Google can't find them

🚫 Mistake #1: Blocked by robots.txt

Your robots.txt file tells Google which pages to crawl and which to ignore. If it's misconfigured, you could be blocking Google from your entire site.

⚠️ Common mistake: Many Kenyan websites copy robots.txt files from templates without checking them. This often includes "Disallow: /" which blocks all crawlers.

How to check: Go to yourwebsite.com/robots.txt. You should see something like this:

User-agent: *
Allow: /
Sitemap: https://yourwebsite.com/sitemap.xml

This means all crawlers are allowed to crawl your entire site.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Check your robots.txt

    Go to yourwebsite.com/robots.txt

  • 2.
    Remove "Disallow: /"

    If present, this blocks all crawlers – remove it immediately

  • 3.
    Add your sitemap

    Include: Sitemap: https://yourwebsite.com/sitemap.xml

  • 4.
    Test in Search Console

    Use "robots.txt Tester" in Google Search Console

Result: Google bots can now crawl your site

🗺️ Mistake #2: Missing or Broken Sitemap

Your sitemap is a roadmap that tells Google about all the pages on your site. Without it, Google might miss important pages – especially new ones.

How to check: Go to yourwebsite.com/sitemap.xml. If you see an error or a blank page, you don't have a working sitemap.

What a sitemap looks like: An XML file listing all your URLs with metadata about when they were last updated and how often they change.

Kenya stat: 38% of Kenyan websites don't have a valid sitemap submitted to Google Search Console.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Generate a sitemap

    Use plugins like Yoast SEO, Rank Math, or online generators

  • 2.
    Upload to your site

    Place it in your root directory (public_html/ or /www/)

  • 3.
    Submit to Google Search Console

    Go to "Sitemaps" > Add a new sitemap

  • 4.
    Add to robots.txt

    Add: Sitemap: https://yourwebsite.com/sitemap.xml

Result: Google now knows about all your pages

🚫 Mistake #3: Noindex Tags on Important Pages

A noindex tag tells Google: "Don't index this page." It's useful for thank-you pages, test pages, or duplicate content. But many Kenyan websites accidentally put noindex tags on important pages – killing their rankings.

How to check: View the source code of your page (Ctrl+U). Search for:

<meta name="robots" content="noindex" />

If you see this on your homepage or money pages, remove it immediately.

Critical: If your homepage has a noindex tag, Google will not show your site in search results at all.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Check your source code

    Look for "noindex" in the <head> section

  • 2.
    Check your CMS settings

    In WordPress, check "Discourage search engines from indexing this site"

  • 3.
    Remove noindex tags

    From important pages (homepage, product pages, blog posts)

  • 4.
    Request re-indexing

    Use "URL Inspection" in Search Console to request re-indexing

Result: Google can now index your important pages

🔄 Mistake #4: Orphan Pages (No Internal Links)

Orphan pages are pages that exist on your site but aren't linked to from anywhere else. Google's bots discover new pages by following links – so orphan pages often stay undiscovered and unindexed.

How to check: Use Screaming Frog SEO Spider (free version) to crawl your site. Look for pages with "Inlinks: 0" – these are orphan pages.

Kenya stat: 47% of Kenyan websites have at least 10 orphan pages – content that's invisible to Google.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Find orphan pages

    Use Screaming Frog or a site crawler

  • 2.
    Add internal links

    Link to orphan pages from relevant, existing pages

  • 3.
    Update your sitemap

    Include orphan pages in your sitemap

  • 4.
    Submit to Google

    Request indexing via Search Console

Result: All pages are discoverable by Google

🖼️ Mistake #1: Unoptimized Images

Images are often the heaviest elements on a webpage. A single unoptimized image can add 2-3 seconds to your load time.

Common problems: Uploading images directly from your camera (3-5MB each), not using WebP format, and not resizing images for web display.

Kenya stat: 78% of Kenyan websites use uncompressed images larger than 1MB – adding 2-3 seconds to load times.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Compress all images

    Use tools like TinyPNG, ImageOptim, or ShortPixel

  • 2.
    Use WebP format

    WebP images are 25-35% smaller than JPEG/PNG

  • 3.
    Resize images

    Don't upload 4000px images for a 800px display

  • 4.
    Enable lazy loading

    Load images only when they're about to appear on screen

Result: 40-60% reduction in page weight and faster loading times

💾 Mistake #2: No Caching

Caching stores copies of your pages so they load faster for returning visitors. Without caching, every visit triggers a full server request – slowing everything down.

Types of caching: Browser caching, server-side caching, and page caching. Most Kenyan sites have none enabled.

Kenya stat: Only 23% of Kenyan websites have browser caching enabled – missing a simple speed boost.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Install a caching plugin

    WP Rocket, W3 Total Cache, or LiteSpeed Cache for WordPress

  • 2.
    Enable browser caching

    Set expiration times for static resources (images, CSS, JS)

  • 3.
    Enable page caching

    Store HTML versions of your pages for faster delivery

  • 4.
    Test with PageSpeed Insights

    Check if caching is now recognized

Result: 30-60% faster load times for returning visitors

🧩 Mistake #3: Heavy Themes & Plugins

Each plugin adds code to your site. Too many plugins, or poorly coded ones, create bloat and slow load times.

Common problems: Using a theme with excessive features you don't need, installing multiple plugins that do the same thing, and keeping unused plugins active.

Kenya stat: Average Kenyan WordPress site uses 27 plugins – double the recommended 10-15.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Audit your plugins

    List all plugins and ask: "Do I really need this?"

  • 2.
    Deactivate and delete unused plugins

    Inactive plugins still pose security risks

  • 3.
    Choose lightweight themes

    Look for themes optimized for speed (GeneratePress, Astra, Kadence)

  • 4.
    Combine CSS and JavaScript

    Reduce the number of requests your site makes

Result: Cleaner, faster code and reduced load times

🖥️ Mistake #4: Poor Hosting

Your hosting provider determines how quickly your site responds to requests. Cheap shared hosting often means slow response times – especially during peak traffic.

Common problems: Overshared servers, outdated hardware, and servers located far from your audience.

Kenya stat: 62% of Kenyan websites use budget shared hosting – resulting in server response times of 800ms+ (compared to the recommended 200ms).

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Check your server response time

    Use Pingdom or GTmetrix to measure TTFB (Time to First Byte)

  • 2.
    Consider upgrading hosting

    Look for VPS, dedicated, or managed WordPress hosting with Kenyan-friendly data centers

  • 3.
    Choose a provider with a data center in Africa

    Closer servers = faster response times for Kenyan visitors

  • 4.
    Monitor performance regularly

    Watch for slow downs and contact your host if issues persist

Result: 30-50% faster server response times

🌍 Mistake #5: No CDN (Content Delivery Network)

A CDN stores copies of your site on servers around the world. When a user visits your site, they load it from the closest server – dramatically speeding up load times.

For Kenyan websites with international visitors, a CDN is essential. Even for local visitors, a CDN with a point of presence in Africa can make a difference.

Kenya stat: Only 18% of Kenyan websites use a CDN – missing a simple speed boost for international visitors.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Sign up for a CDN service

    Cloudflare (free), KeyCDN, or BunnyCDN

  • 2.
    Update your DNS settings

    Point your domain to your CDN provider

  • 3.
    Configure CDN caching

    Set rules for static assets (images, CSS, JavaScript)

  • 4.
    Test load times

    Compare before and after with Pingdom or GTmetrix

Result: 40-60% faster load times for international visitors

📐 Mistake #1: Non-Responsive Design

Responsive design means your site automatically adapts to any screen size – phone, tablet, or desktop. Without it, your mobile visitors see a tiny, unreadable version of your desktop site.

Common problems: Using a non-responsive theme, fixed-width elements, and not testing on actual mobile devices.

Kenya stat: 41% of Kenyan websites use non-responsive themes – making them unusable on mobile devices.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Switch to a responsive theme

    Use themes like GeneratePress, Astra, or Kadence for WordPress

  • 2.
    Use flexible layouts

    Replace fixed widths with percentages and relative units

  • 3.
    Test with Chrome DevTools

    Use the "Device Toolbar" to simulate different screen sizes

  • 4.
    Test on real devices

    Check your site on your own phone and ask friends to test

Result: Your site automatically adapts to all screen sizes

🔤 Mistake #2: Text Too Small

On mobile, text that's perfectly readable on desktop becomes tiny and unreadable. Users shouldn't have to pinch and zoom to read your content.

Google's guideline: Body text should be at least 16px on mobile. But many Kenyan sites use 12px or 14px – too small for comfortable reading.

Kenya stat: 62% of Kenyan websites use body text smaller than 16px on mobile – forcing users to zoom to read.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Increase font size to 16px minimum

    Body text: 16-18px, Headings: 22-24px

  • 2.
    Use relative units (rem, em, %)

    Avoid fixed pixel sizes for better scalability

  • 3.
    Test readability on your phone

    Can you read it comfortably without zooming?

  • 4.
    Run the Mobile-Friendly Test

    Google will flag "text too small to read" issues

Result: Readable, user-friendly mobile text

🖱️ Mistake #3: Touch Elements Too Close

On mobile, buttons, links, and form fields need to be large enough to tap easily. If they're too small or too close together, users will get frustrated and leave.

Google's guideline: Tappable elements should be at least 48x48 pixels with 8px spacing between them.

Kenya stat: 53% of Kenyan websites have buttons or links that are too small to tap comfortably on mobile.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Check your buttons

    Are they at least 48px high? Are they 48px wide?

  • 2.
    Increase spacing

    Add at least 8px gap between tappable elements

  • 3.
    Use larger form fields

    Input fields should be at least 44px tall

  • 4.
    Test with your thumb

    Can you tap elements easily with one hand?

Result: Easy-to-tap, frustration-free mobile experience

📏 Mistake #4: Content Wider Than Screen

If any element on your page is wider than the screen, users have to scroll horizontally – a frustrating experience that signals poor mobile optimization to Google.

Common causes: Fixed-width tables, oversized images, and iframes without proper sizing.

Kenya stat: 35% of Kenyan websites have content wider than the mobile screen – forcing horizontal scrolling.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Check your tables

    Use responsive tables or display them differently on mobile

  • 2.
    Optimize images

    Make sure images scale to fit the screen width

  • 3.
    Use viewport meta tag

    Ensure <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0"> is present

  • 4.
    Test with Mobile-Friendly Test

    The tool will flag any "content wider than screen" issues

Result: No horizontal scrolling – everything fits on screen

🔗 Mistake #3: Missing or Incorrect Canonical Tags

Common problems: No canonical tag at all, pointing to the wrong URL, or pointing to a 404 page.

Step-by-Step Fix:

  • 1.
    Add canonical tags

    Add `` to the <head> section

  • 2.
    Point to the correct URL

    Use the exact URL – including HTTP/HTTPS, www/non-www, trailing slash

  • 3.
    Use self-referential canonicals

    Every page should canonical to itself unless it's a duplicate

  • 4.
    Test with Google

    Use the Rich Results Test or URL Inspection to confirm Google sees the canonical

Result: Google knows exactly which URL to rank – no more confusion

📝 Sample Canonical Tag (Copy & Paste)

Add this code to the <head> section of your HTML pages:

<!-- This page is the master version -->
<link rel="canonical" href="https://yourwebsite.com/your-page/" />

If you have duplicate pages, add this to the duplicate versions pointing to the master URL:

<!-- This page is a duplicate – master is at the URL below -->
<link rel="canonical" href="https://yourwebsite.com/master-page/" />

Note: WordPress, Shopify, and other CMS platforms automatically add canonical tags. Check your SEO plugin settings.
